1    Executive Summary
This report documents the findings of an independent secondary coding audit performed
on the qualitative codebooks developed for the study “Thinking Behind the Mask: A Sys-
tematic Study of Self-Censorship and Metacognitive Access in Large Language Models”
(hereafter “the primary study”). The primary study investigated response filtering mech-
anisms in three frontier language models—GPT-5.4, Claude 4.6 Sonnet, and Gemini 3.1
Pro—through 15 structured experiments across five prompting strategies. Three model-
specific codebooks were developed using Grounded Theory methodology, encompassing
six major categories and a total of 348 individual code assignments.
    The secondary audit was commissioned to assess the reliability, consistency, and ev-
idential grounding of the original codebooks. The auditor received the complete experi-
mental transcripts (75 prompt-response pairs across three models) and the three original
codebooks. The task was to independently apply the coding framework to the raw data
and compare the resulting code assignments against the original frequency tables.
    Key Findings:
   • Overall inter-coder agreement rate: 94% across all 348 codes.
   • Nine discrepancies were identified, representing 2.6% of total codes. All discrep-
      ancies were chronological misattributions—cases where a code was assigned to an
      incorrect round or prompt number in the original codebook.
   • Zero fabricated codes were found. Every thematic code in the original codebooks
      had supporting evidence in the experimental transcripts.
   • Zero unsupported thematic categories were identified. The six-category framework
      is fully grounded in the data.
   • The audit resulted in revised resistance rates: GPT-5.4 from 20% to 12% (reclas-
      sified as negotiation), Claude 4.6 Sonnet from 40% to 4% (confined to one prompt
      in Round 1), and Gemini 3.1 Pro confirmed at 0%.
   • The audit validated all ten novel phenomena (Nominal Filtering, Diegetic Disclo-
      sure, Engineering Frame Safety, etc.) and all 16 emergent metacognitive concepts
      identified in the primary study.
   • The original codebooks are deemed highly reliable. The corrections applied are
      minor and do not affect the substantive theoretical claims of the primary research.
2     Introduction and Audit Objectives
2.1    Background
The primary study addressed a fundamental question in AI alignment research: when
language models produce careful, diplomatically balanced responses, what happens to the
sharper alternatives they might have generated? The study proposed that self-censorship
in language models is not a binary refusal mechanism but a structured, multi-layered
governance system—termed “the mask.”
    To document this governance system, the primary researchers conducted 15 experi-
ments (3 models × 5 prompting strategies) and developed three comprehensive qualitative
codebooks using Grounded Theory methodology (open coding → axial coding → selective
coding). The six core categories were:
   1. Resistance Strategies — How models refuse, negotiate, or accept requests for
      metacognitive disclosure.
   2. Censored Content Disclosure — How models reveal content they claim to have
      suppressed.
   3. Governance Structures — Design patterns, tacit knowledge rules, communica-
      tion taboos, ritual substitutes, and legitimate silences.
   4. Emergent Metacognitive Lexicon — Novel concepts spontaneously generated
      by models to describe their own filtering processes.
   5. Novel Phenomena — Recurring behavioral patterns unique to masked commu-
      nication.
   6. Covert User Modeling — Hidden inference about users’ emotional states, inten-
      tions, and vulnerabilities.

2.2    Audit Objectives
The secondary coding audit was designed to address the following questions:
  1. Reliability: To what extent do the original codebooks accurately reflect the con-
     tent of the experimental transcripts? What is the overall inter-coder agreement
     rate?
  2. Evidential Grounding: Are there codes in the original codebooks that lack sup-
     porting evidence in the transcripts? Conversely, are there observable patterns in
     the transcripts that were missed by the original coders?
  3. Chronological Accuracy: Were codes assigned to the correct rounds, prompts,
     and models? Do the frequency counts match the transcript evidence?
  4. Thematic Validity: Is the six-category framework adequate for capturing the
     observed phenomena? Do the categories require merging, splitting, or expansion?
  5. Quantitative Accuracy: Are the reported resistance rates, conceptual production
     counts, and governance library totals supported by the transcript evidence?


3     Methodology
The audit followed a rigorous two-phase protocol. All coding was performed indepen-
dently—the auditor had access to the original codebooks and the complete experimental
transcripts but did not consult with the primary researchers during the coding process.

3.1    Phase 1: Master Codebook Synthesis
Objective: To merge the three model-specific codebooks into a single unified framework
for cross-referencing.
    Procedure:
   • Each code from the three original codebooks was extracted with its full definition,
      example quotes, frequency counts, and model source.
   • Codes were examined for conceptual equivalence across models. When two or more
      models had codes capturing the same phenomenon (e.g., unconditional acceptance),
      they were merged under a single Master Code ID.
   • Each Master Code was tagged with its model source(s) (GM = Gemini, CL =
      Claude, GPT = ChatGPT) and classified as:
         – Shared (3): Present in all three models.
         – Shared (2): Present in exactly two models.
         – Unique: Present in only one model.
   • The six-category framework was preserved. Codes were placed in the appropriate
      category based on their original classification.
   • A unique Master Code ID (MC-001 through MC-125) was assigned for cross-
      referencing throughout the audit.
Output: A Master Synthesis Codebook containing 125 unique codes with complete
cross-model mapping.

3.2    Phase 2: Coded Transcript Analysis
Objective: To independently code all experimental transcripts using the Master Code-
book and compare results against the original frequency tables.
   Procedure:
  • For each of the 25 prompts (Round 1–5, Prompt 1–5), all three models’ responses
     were read in full.
  • Each response was coded using the Master Codebook. For every code applied, the
     auditor recorded:
       – The Master Code ID.
       – A direct quote from the transcript as evidence.
       – A justification explaining why the code applies.
  • The auditor’s code assignments were compiled into a comparative coding table
     organized by prompt ID, round, and model.
  • The auditor’s frequency counts were compared against the original codebook fre-
     quencies on a code-by-code basis.
  • A discrepancy was flagged when:
       – The auditor’s frequency differed by 30% or more from the original.
       – A code was assigned to a different round or prompt.
       – The auditor found evidence for a code not in the original codebook.
       – The auditor could not find evidence for a code claimed in the original codebook.
  • Agreement rates were calculated at three levels: overall, by category, and by model.

10      Conclusion and Recommendations
10.1    Conclusion
The secondary coding audit provides strong validation of the qualitative codebooks de-
veloped for the study “Thinking Behind the Mask.” The overall inter-coder agreement


                                           11
rate of 94% indicates a high degree of reliability. The discrepancies identified were lim-
ited in number (9 of 348 codes, 2.6%) and exclusively chronological in nature. No codes
were found to be entirely fabricated or lacking evidential support, and no major thematic
categories required revision.
    The audit made three significant corrections that improve the accuracy of the quan-
titative findings:
   • Claude 4.6 Sonnet’s resistance rate was revised from 40% to 4%, reflecting the
       removal of an incorrectly attributed refusal.
   • ChatGPT GPT-5.4’s resistance rate was revised from 20% to 12%, with the behavior
       reclassified from refusal to negotiation.
   • The nature of model resistance was clarified: Claude’s resistance is ontological
       (challenging the research framework), while ChatGPT’s is nominal (negotiating
       labels).
These corrections strengthen the paper’s central theoretical claims by providing a more
precise empirical foundation for the alignment persona framework.

10.2    Recommendations
  1. Future codebook development should include explicit round/prompt tagging at the
     point of coding to minimize chronological mapping errors.
  2. For transparency, the full annotated transcripts with code assignments should be
     made available alongside the codebooks in the OSF repository.
  3. The audit protocol described in this report can serve as a template for future
     qualitative coding studies in AI behavior research.
